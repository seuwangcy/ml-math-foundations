# About this project

## Why I'm writing this

I'm a self-taught ML practitioner. I've read papers, taken courses, built
models — but I keep hitting a wall: when the math gets dense, I lose the thread.
I can follow a derivation line by line, but I can't *see* it.

So I'm writing a workbook. The thesis is simple:

> **You don't really understand something until you can explain it.**

Not explain it to an expert — explain it to yourself, six months from now, when
you've forgotten everything.

## What this is not

- ❌ Not a textbook. Textbooks aim for completeness; I aim for understanding.
- ❌ Not a polished reference. Expect drafts, dead ends, and self-corrections.
- ❌ Not a one-way street. If you spot a mistake, please open an issue.

## What this is

- ✅ A **workbook**: working through ideas in my own words, in public
- ✅ **Focused on intuition first**: a picture before a formula
- ✅ **Annotated with ML use cases**: every idea tied to where it shows up
- ✅ **Iterative**: notes get rewritten as I understand better

## How I work

- One note at a time. Quality over quantity.
- Pick the topic I'm currently most confused by, not the most "important".
- Write the intuition *before* the math. If I can't, I don't understand it yet.
- Read what I wrote a week later and rewrite anything that's unclear.
